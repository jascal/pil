## Read (descriptive, no overclaim)

1. DEEP COMPUTATION IS THE NORM. mean k*/L ∈ [0.80, 0.97] everywhere: most tokens' decodes are not locked
   until ~the top of the stack. This is the graded confirmation of mass≠causation / the decode circuit —
   the whole stack is causally load-bearing for the typical token; the late-MLP attribution is a readout
   over a residual that the deep stack had to build.

2. A REAL BUT MINORITY EARLY-RESOLVED TAIL. shallow(≤0.5L) is 0–14%: a minority of tokens are causally
   locked by the bottom half (the candidate "retrievable/easy" tokens — likely high-prior / induction-copy;
   per-token typing is a follow-up using the --converge-out dump + vocab).

3. WITHIN PYTHIA (controlled arch), the depth FRACTION falls with scale: 410m 0.85 → 1b 0.82 → 1.4b 0.80,
   shallow-fraction 10% → 14%. Deeper/bigger Pythia locks the decode at a *smaller fraction* of its depth
   (more capacity spent on late refinement/redundancy). The 70m/160m points (0.96–0.97) are partly a
   GRANULARITY artifact — at L=6/12 the measure is coarsely quantised and removing the single readout layer
   flips most tokens, so compare only L≥16.

4. NOT A CLEAN UNIVERSAL LAW. Qwen is non-monotone (0.5B 0.91, 1.5B 0.87, 3B 0.93) — Qwen-3B (36 layers)
   bucks the within-Pythia trend, locking late again. So "depth-fraction shrinks with scale" is an
   architecture-dependent tendency, not a law. Raw data above; verdict stays descriptive.
