# PIC rule learner — classic tabular ML benchmarks (2026-07-02)

Fixed 70/30 stratified splits (seed 0). Baselines on the same splits: HistGradientBoosting
(the incumbent for tabular; sees RAW floats) and LogisticRegression (standardized raw
floats) via sklearn; the MLP and PIL consume identical quantile-binned atoms (16
bins/feature, categoricals pass through; encoding in experiments/tabular_bench.py).
"PIL hard" = Boolean-gate accuracy — the number the exported Soufflé program reproduces
(verified exact on breast_cancer and wine test sets: agreement 1.00, 0 undecided).
certified@0.25 = PIC §5.5 fraction of test decisions provably robust to per-token
clause-weight drift δ=0.25.

Reading (honest):
- **GBT wins everywhere** — as expected: it is the tuned incumbent and sees raw floats
  while PIL sees 16-level bins. The gap is 2.4–7.5 points.
- **PIL beats LogReg and the MLP on adult** — the largest and most "real" dataset (mixed
  categorical/numeric): 0.843 vs 0.821 / 0.808. On the small numeric datasets the nearly
  linear structure favours LogReg (wine is linearly separable: LogReg 1.0).
- **Ordinal literals were load-bearing**: wine 0.833 → 0.944 the moment `x[o] ≤ b` tests
  (tree-style splits; exported as `tok(I,o,C), C <= c`) joined the literal vocabulary.
- What PIL uniquely delivers at these accuracies: a runnable, certified Datalog program
  (rules with explicit bodies; Soufflé-verified decode), per-decision §5.5 robustness
  certificates (adult: 90% of decisions certified at δ=0.25), exact per-rule ablations,
  and free abstention semantics — none of which GBT/MLP provide.
- Un-pulled levers for closing the GBT gap: finer bins / learned bin edges, per-class
  targeted births, bagged programs, longer training (adult = 199s CPU here), and raw-value
  threshold literals (skip binning entirely).

Raw rows: results/tabular_bench.jsonl. Dataset/baseline materializer:
scratchpad/make_tabular.py (run under a sklearn venv).
